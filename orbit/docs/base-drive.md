# Moiré Orbit build notes

Open **Moiré Orbit** from the original generator at `http://localhost:8080/`. **Centre Spindle 3D** opens the sibling Smooth viewer on port 8090. In Moiré Orbit, **Disc diameter** sets the upper cradle and surround, **Pulley cutaway** sections a drive, and **Mount and backlight** previews the two installation options and the lighting add-on. See the [parts checklist](parts-checklist.md) for sources, quantities and the power budget.

Use the top navigation to carry the disc pattern, colours, starting angles and layer count between the two local viewers. Moiré Orbit uses the transferred pattern for visual preview. Check and export the geometry in the appropriate fabrication workflow before printing a customised disc.

**Print layout** spreads the frame, enclosure, discs, pulleys, cogs and keepers on the floor for inspection. The three-disc printed profile shows the six exported shell sections. For other stacks, export the matching shell sections from the parametric CAD. In the slicer, use the frame's bed orientation with support under the rear rail, and keep support removable from the bearing pockets.

## Hardware reference

The [AliExpress item](https://www.aliexpress.com/item/1005009331799965.html) appears in a [build BOM](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/EmilPtr/LimeArm-MKI/main/LimeArm%20MKI%20BOM.csv) as a 28BYJ-48 with ULN2003. The seller page did not expose the selected variant or dimensional drawing. The sibling [Smooth firmware notes](../../Moire-Effect-Generator-Smooth/firmware/README.md) specify the stock 5 V 28BYJ-48, one ULN2003 board per motor, and an ESP32-S3 SuperMini for three motors. The modeled can is Ø28.6 × 19.5 mm, the shaft is offset by 8 mm with Ø5 mm and two flats 3 mm apart, and the ear holes are nominally Ø4.2 mm on 35 mm centres. Use calipers to check ear pitch, hole diameter, shaft projection and flats on the actual motors. Check the voltage marked on the motors before applying power.

The motors mount with their cans pointing into the foot and shafts toward the rear drive plane. Each motor uses an open timing belt with two equal 16T pulleys at 20 mm nominal centres. Start with one 36-tooth, 72 mm pitch-length, 2 mm pitch × 3 mm wide timing belt. The rendered pulley meshes are the same geometry as the printed prototypes. The belt ratio is 1:1, so the 12T pinion and 124T rim give **10.333:1** overall. Check fit, tracking and reversal with one motor and one belt before building all three. [Timing belt drawing](https://shop.sdp-si.com/media/pdf/80502136.pdf) · [16T 2GT pulley reference](https://shop.sdp-si.com/media/pdf/80502140.pdf)

### Printable pulley pair and belt

| Feature | Nominal size |
| --- | --- |
| Timing profile | 16 teeth per pulley, 2 mm pitch, Ø10.19 mm pitch circle |
| Flanges and face | Ø12.10 mm flange OD, 0.45 mm flange thickness, 3.30 mm clear belt face |
| Assembly width | 4.20 mm belt section plus 2.30 mm screw boss, 6.50 mm total |
| Motor hub | Ø5.15 mm double-D opening, 3.10 mm across flats |
| Spindle hub | Ø3.16 mm D opening, flat 1.13 mm from centre |
| Set screw | Ø1.60 mm pilot to tap M2, screw nose seats against the shaft flat |

1. Measure the round, flats and usable shaft projection. Set `motor_round` and `motor_across_flats` in the [pulley CAD](../cad/moire-captive-drive.scad) if your motor differs. The `motor_pulley` and `spindle_pulley` outputs stand on their lower flange for printing. The section outputs are for inspection.
2. Print one of each in PLA+ with a 0.4 mm nozzle and a 0.16 mm layer height. Check the upper flange overhang and keep support out of the running profile where possible. Clear the bore and pilot, tap M2, and leave the screw recessed or flush with the hub flat. Press-fit an insert only after enlarging the boss and checking the radial clearance.
3. Use one endless **72-2GT-3** belt per motor. Confirm 36 teeth and 3 mm width on any spare belt. A household rubber band can turn a smooth-pulley mock-up by hand. Elastic stretch and slip change the phase when the motor reverses. [Gates Unitta 2GT size table](https://www.unitta.co.jp/products/industry/power/gt/gtbelt2) · [Exact 72-2GT-3 listing](https://www.axel-gl.com/en/asone/d/68-5448-95/)
4. Fit the spindle pulley, place the belt over both pulleys with the motor pulley loose, and slide the motor pulley onto the motor shaft. Align the flanges with a straightedge. Swing each outer motor about its inner screw, and slide the centre motor along its slots. Adjust until the belt tracks and stays engaged during a slow reversal. Tighten both mount screws with the motor face seated flat. Trial the nominal 20 mm centre setting with one belt before building every station. [SDP/SI timing belt installation guide](https://shop.sdp-si.com/products/synchronous-drive-belts-pulleys-cables/timing-belt-design-and-installation-suggestions.html)
5. Keep the back panel off while setting belt tension. Turn the drive by hand and check pulley, screw and wire clearance through a full turn before fitting the panel.

### Screw access and insert choices

On A and B, the inner screw sets a fixed pivot in a Ø3.25 mm hole. Leave it just snug, swing the outer ear along the 3.40 mm curved slot, then tighten both screws. C's two short slots are printed at the height for that size. Start with **M3 × 8 screws, 6 mm OD washers and M3 nuts**. From the rear, the stack is the screw head, washer, 2.30 mm deck, motor ear, second washer and nut. Hold the nut on the motor side and use the lowest tension that prevents skipping.

Use the through-bolts for the first carrier. For fixed shell and guide locations, use a captive nut, an insert in a sufficiently thick boss, or an intentionally sized pilot hole for a thread-forming screw. Identify the M2/M3 thread, outside diameter and length of each loose insert. Print a little test boss for that specific insert before setting a production pilot. The motor ears take clearance screws, not threaded holes printed to match an unknown insert.

The viewer uses A for the front disc (L1), B for L2 and C for the rear disc (L3), and treats positive disc rpm as clockwise seen from the front. It uses the stock firmware's 12 rpm motor-output cap, not a tested torque or speed rating. The default 180-second cycle gives disc speeds of +0.333, −0.667 and +1.000 rpm, requiring motor-output speeds of −3.444, +6.889 and −10.333 rpm. The firmware's `ratio`, `l1`, `l2` and `l3` fields can represent these settings. The viewer does not modify firmware or device settings.

## Drop-in cartridges and shell

The outer spindles are at 228° and 314° in the S100 layout, 4° farther out per side. Their spacing is 74.20 mm on every cradle. A and B each use one driven cog and one free idler on the opposite outer spindle. C uses the centre drive cog and an idler on each outer spindle. The centre spindle uses a narrow spacer tube across the A and B lanes. Each cog has two integral tapered lips with a nominal 0.15 mm clearance per face. The 1.5 mm gap between discs fits the skeleton's 1.10 mm guide tabs with 0.20 mm clearance per side. Each half of the cap carries one smooth stop per disc, with 0.50 mm radial clearance over the teeth. Check seating and hand rotation with the fitted cap in place.

The [structure CAD](../cad/s100-skeleton.scad) exports the common electronics foot as `common_base` and the size-specific frame as `cradle`. The rear rail shares a flat face with the motor deck. Two gusseted guide posts, cross-ties near the outer bearings and Ø10 mm bearing pods stiffen the guide frame. The two locating toes drop into the foot at X = ±48.5 mm, with one pocket relieved along X. Fit two M2 × 5 countersunk screws from underneath. For a diameter change, unplug the motors and remove the two recessed screws to lift the drive frame away from the electronics foot. Transfer the drive hardware to the matching frame, discs and shell. Select `orientation = "bed"` in OpenSCAD for slicer-oriented exports.

The S100 window is Ø92 mm. The circular surround and lift-off lid share a Ø124.9 mm outside diameter. The plain 1.4 mm front skin covers the recessed spindle ends. The compact foot measures **113 × 38.35 mm** in plan. The enclosure is **117.2 mm wide at its motor shoulders** and **44.5 mm high**, excluding the soft feet. The A and B belts splay out 30° from vertical. C's belt angle comes in from 67° on S100 to 50° on S300 to give its outer screw and washer room beside motor A. Each pulley pair stays in one plane at 20.00 mm nominal centres.

The 35 × 32 mm ULN2003 driver boards sit sockets-up on 1.55 mm standoffs. The quarter-turned C card leaves a 1.9 mm nominal gap on either side. Fit the intact motor plugs under the frame before securing the boards. The upright ESP32 slides out on the right after removing the side panel and its retainers. Its connector face is 0.60 mm inside the shoulder, behind a 9.2 × 16.8 mm opening. Use **Wiring & parts** to trace the connections. Confirm the actual plug bodies, wire bends and antenna placement before closing the case. The [structure STLs](../cad/skeleton/) include the common foot and all size-specific frames. [ESP32-S3 antenna guidance](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-hardware-design-guidelines/en/latest/esp32s3/pcb-layout-design.html)

The [case panel CAD](../cad/moire-case-panels.scad) produces a 1.8 mm front cover, 1.2 mm rear cover and 1.6 mm side returns. The size-specific 0.8 mm apron overlaps the front cover from behind and closes the view of the motor shoulders. Print the flat panels visible face down. Dry-fit the front locators in the side pockets. Lower the rear cover's two keys into the foot and lift it by about 2 mm to remove it. Print the side return twice and mirror one in the slicer. Check the support placement and dry-fit both USB-C cables before fastening the panels. [Panel STLs](../cad/case-panels/)

### Modular shell trial

[The parametric shell CAD](../cad/moire-modular-shell.scad) splits the circular fascia and outer wall into four fixed sections and two lid sections. Lay each front face flat on the bed so the wall builds upward from the ring face. The fixed divisions follow the drive locations for the selected diameter. Every glue seam has a pocket for an asymmetric bow-tie key inserted from the back. It indexes the pieces in both directions and keeps glue off the visible face.

Dry-fit [the joint coupon](../cad/modular-shell/s250/coupon.stl) first and adjust `key_clearance` from the 0.20 mm per-side starting value if needed. Set the fixed sections in order 1–4 on a flat surface. Join the two lid sections separately. Fit the matching keys from the rear, check the assembled curve against the cradle, then apply a small amount of CA to each fitted key on the back. Leave both joints between the lid and fixed shell free so the lid can lift out. The sector STLs cover the round front and outer wall for three printed discs. For other stacks, set `disc_count` and `cartridge_width` in the [shell CAD](../cad/moire-modular-shell.scad) before exporting. The neck, rear panel and lid locating hardware need a bench fit with those parts before a full shell print. ## Optional backlight cassette

The [cassette CAD](../cad/moire-diffuser.scad) provides four printable frame quadrants with stepped joints. Match the pieces to the sculpture size. The face diameters are **87.9 mm (S100), 137.5 mm (S150), 236.7 mm (S250) and 287.1 mm (S300)**. The screen sits 8.65 mm behind the back of the thickest rear cartridge in the nominal model.

1. Print `frame-1` through `frame-4` face down from the [matching size folder](../cad/diffuser/). Dry-fit them flat before gluing the stepped joints. Print four 1.20 mm white `face-quarter` pieces or cut a single 2 mm opal acrylic face to the diameter above.
2. For a passive white screen, use the [long face retainers](../cad/diffuser/retainer-face.stl). For lighting, the frame fits a 2 mm opal face, a 3 mm edge-light guide and a thin white reflector. Fit the [short lighting retainers](../cad/diffuser/retainer.stl) on the back edge and shim the stack if needed. Use a small brim on the retainers if bed adhesion is marginal. Follow the light maker's mounting and thermal instructions and measure its current on a separate supply.
3. With the sculpture unplugged, off the wall and the discs out, hold the cassette centred behind the cradle. Transfer the two ear locations, then drill 3.4 mm through the rear cradle rail only. The datums are 200° and 340° at the tooth-tip radius plus 5.0 mm, measured from +X in the front view. Fit two M3 × 12 studs from the front of the rear rail and secure them with thin M3 seat nuts. The cassette slips over those posts and seats against the nuts. Add rear thumb nuts to hold it in place. Remove those two thumb nuts to lift the cassette away without dismantling the drive.

Use **Mount and backlight → Mount inspection → Inspect diffuser mount** to see the installed posts and removable cassette. The lit preview is a static approximation of an opal screen. [Perspex diffuser sheet](https://www.perspex.co.uk/materials/perspex-cast-acrylic/diffuse/)

### Gear fit verification

The Orbit rim and pinion both use 0.10 mm tooth thinning at their pitch circles, giving 0.20 mm nominal tangential backlash. The tooth-tip-to-root allowance is 0.20 mm radially. The two tapered lips leave 0.15 mm per side around a matched cartridge. The headless screw in the driven pinion seats beneath the tooth root.

All four sizes cleared a full tooth pitch at each of the three station angles in 720 steps per station. The smallest gap between unloaded tooth profiles was 0.0906 mm at the indexed running phase. A centre-distance sweep 0.10 mm closer still left at least 0.048 mm at that phase.

Aim to hold each running centre within ±0.10 mm of nominal after accounting for bore play, carrier location and rim runout. The stock bearing pocket and idler bore include FDM allowances. Use the `fit_coupon` in the [cradle CAD](../cad/s100-skeleton.scad) to choose a snug outer-race seat. Fit the idler bores to the measured shaft and check play under the weight of a disc. Verify tooth engagement over a full hand-turned revolution before adding belt tension or motor power. Use the 12T Orbit drive parts and matching size interface above. The earlier `moire-drive-v2.scad` prototype uses a different 18T drive.

Both printable timing pulleys have the same 16T profile. The 36T belt loops around them at 20.00 mm centres. The 3.00 mm belt leaves 0.15 mm clearance on each side of the 3.30 mm face. The browser shows the teeth moving with both pulleys. The printed belt profiles cleared their pulley grooves through a pitch in the nominal model, including entry and exit. Verify stretch and tracking on the actual print under light tension and reversal.

## What turns and what stays still

- Each shaft carries one locked rim cog and one locked belt pulley. The cog has a round bore and an M1.6 side screw that lands on a short flat. The spindle pulley has a single-D bore and an M2 screw. The separate motor pulley has a double-D bore and an M2 screw. Align the screws to the flats and leave the free-running sections of the steel shaft round.
- In the three-disc build each outer shaft carries two free idlers. A Ø3.30 mm starting bore lets each idler rotate independently on a smooth Ø3 mm journal. Small 0.20 mm thrust shims separate adjacent hubs. The 0.30 mm gap between cog bodies leaves 0.10 mm nominal axial play after the shim. Choose shims after measuring the printed wheels. On C, fit a smooth spacer tube from the drive cog to the front bearing. The nominal length is 8.75 mm for printed cartridges and 11.15 mm for hybrid cartridges. Check length after assembling the measured stack.
- The stationary supports are **MR63ZZ (3 × 6 × 2.5 mm)**. The pocket and keeper retain each outer race. Use straight Ø3 mm round stock with a short flat under the driven cog's screw and a D-flat along the length of the rear pulley hub. Leave the bearing and idler journals round. The rear pulley and front retaining clip locate the shaft axially through small spacers that bear on the inner races. Determine length, flats and groove from the measured stack and chosen clip. **Pulley cutaway** shows the reference arrangement.
- A smooth Ø3 mm ground shaft is the simplest stock to cut for the rotating spindle. Use two shallow flats for the drive cog and rear pulley screws. Annealed silver steel makes those flats easier to file. A precision shoulder screw can supply the front head if the smooth Ø3 mm grip spans the entire stack and its head fits behind the fascia. Keep threaded sections outside the bearings and idler bores. The housing hardware can use regular threaded bolts. [Steel rod datasheet](https://assets.rs-online.com/image/upload/v1706620547/Datasheets/e2a63154fc489139b1e193fb4d55803d.pdf)
- The [printed sleeve trial](../cad/skeleton/sleeve-bushing.stl) fits the same pocket as the MR63ZZ and uses the same outer keeper. It has a nominal 6.08 mm outside diameter, 2.50 mm width and 3.25 mm printed bore before cleaning. Try one paired set on a smooth rod. Compare starting drag, lateral play and wear with a metal-bearing station under belt tension.
- Print a pair of pulleys and one cog set from [the editable CAD](../cad/moire-captive-drive.scad) or use the [STL folder](../cad/drive-parts/). The nominal motor bore is 5.15 mm round and 3.10 mm across the flats. Each pulley hub has a flat face and a 1.60 mm M2 pilot. Chase the pilot by hand, start with light screw pressure and confirm the screw seats flush. A heat-set insert needs a larger boss and a new clearance check. [Heat-set insert dimensions](https://cnckitchen.com/s/CNCKitchen_Heat-Set-Insert-Dimensions-and-Design-Guidelines.pdf)

Try the [0.28 mm radial gap](../cad/printed-drive/idler-gap-028.stl) first, or the [0.34 mm radial gap](../cad/printed-drive/idler-gap-034.stl) if the captured wheel fuses. The print-in-place assembly is a single-slot trial. Both samples sliced, with warnings at the small hub and overhangs. ## Quiet operation

The stock 5 V project uses ULN2003 half-stepping and a 12 rpm motor-output cap. The preview's **Gentle drift** uses the same smoothly varying waveform as the firmware with 25% variation and the firmware's 0.3 disc-rpm/s ramp. It adjusts the minimum nominal turn time so speed peaks fit under the cap. It does not configure the controller. The [28BYJ-48 datasheet](https://altronics.cl/uploads/28BYJ-48.pdf) specifies under 35 dB for the motor at 120 Hz, unloaded and measured at 10 cm. A quiet band for the assembled sculpture cannot be inferred from that test. Run one motor unloaded at several speeds, then repeat with the gear carrier, then the disc. Starter comparison points are 3, 6 and 9 motor rpm. Listen for gear tooth noise and housing resonance, and check that there are no missed steps or disc lifts. Keep gears and their journals on a stiff, accurately located carrier. Start by isolating the whole base from the shelf with rubber feet, and leave wire and cover clearance so they cannot buzz against a motor. Consider the modified bipolar/TMC2209 option only if motor stepping remains the main noise source after the mechanical checks.

Bench-check the printed tolerances, loaded belt tracking, motor torque, sound, wiring and lid travel. Start with one motor, one belt pair and one slot with a rim sector. For the larger sizes, also check that the common foot is stable with the full stack fitted.
